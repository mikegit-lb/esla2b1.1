Coach's corner

Make the 90 minutes count.

This page is the plan, not the lesson. Read the room, cut an activity if energy drops, and always protect the last ten minutes for speaking without support.

How to run a session

  1. Start talking before books open. Ask the warm-up question to the learner directly, then let them ask you.
  2. Teach words in threes. Present, make an example together, then the learner makes one about their own life.
  3. Set a visible timer. Short, timed speaking rounds create more talk than open-ended chat.
  4. Correct less, note more. Write errors down, choose two to fix at the end, and ignore the rest.
  5. Finish with a recording. The fluency sprint is the proof of progress. Keep every recording.
  6. Build one speaking layer per session. A good layer has a phrase bank learners can borrow, two or three sentence frames that shape the turn, and a short task that forces them to use today's words instead of the first word that comes to mind.
  7. Drill the mouth, not just the meaning. A word is not learned until the learner can say it at normal speed in a real sentence; a one-minute repetition drill beats ten silent looks at the card.
  8. Tie vocabulary to speaking as early as possible. As soon as a word is introduced, use it in a question, a frame or a lie-and-truth task; words that only appear on the card rarely survive the week.
  9. Teach grammar in one minute, then use it. Read the form, give one example, and send learners straight into a question from that theme. The card is a tool, not a lecture.
  10. Send the reading home. The reading assignment is homework: read once for meaning, answer the ten questions, then check the key. Bring two questions back to class.
